Method

Three pillars, one contract

We don't sell hives on their own. Here's why that matters.

A honey bee colony is livestock. Adding colonies to a landscape with no forage moves the problem rather than solving it, and competes with the wild bees already there. So colonies, habitat, and measurement travel together in every program we run.

C · Colonies

Managed apiaries

A named beekeeper, fourteen visits from April through November, documented varroa monitoring, all treatments and feed, hurricane protocol, state registration handled. You keep every ounce of honey.

H · Habitat

Native forage

Meadow conversion, hedgerows, borders, and mowing and pesticide protocols designed for your soil and light. Planned in winter, planted in spring, managed on a calendar you keep.

M · Measurement

Ecological baseline

Wild bee transect surveys, pollen analysis identifying the plant species actually in use, and a pesticide residue panel. Delivered as a report you can hand to a certifier, a board, or a buyer.

Our limits

What we won't do

The things we
turn down.

Most of our competitors will sell you as many hives as you'll buy.

We publish a limit instead — because a client who later learns their program was ecologically hollow is a client we've lost, and a landscape we've made worse.

We cap colony density per acre of assessed forage.

If your property can't feed the colonies you've asked for, we'll tell you the number it can support and place that many. This is not negotiable, including when you'd rather we placed more.

We won't place colonies against protected habitat.

No siting within our published buffer of Central Pine Barrens core, town preserves, or land-trust holdings without an ecological assessment that clears it first.

We don't sell a hives-only product.

Every program includes habitat. If you want colonies and nothing else, we're the wrong company, and we'll refer you to a good local beekeeper who'll do it well.

We document the wild bees before we add honey bees.

You get a baseline whether or not the number flatters the plan. If the survey says your site is better served by habitat alone, that's what we'll recommend.

We walk the property, read the light and the soil, check the setbacks against your town code, and tell you what the ground can actually support. You get a written siting assessment and a recommended program. If the answer is that you don't need us, that's in the report too.
